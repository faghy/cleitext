# WordPress Importer

The official Cleitext plugin to move a WordPress site in from the admin, without the command line. It is not in the base package: install it from **Plugins → Add New** (catalog), or download `cleitext-plugin-wordpress-importer-<version>.zip` from the Releases page and upload it in **Plugins → Upload Plugin**.

## How to use it

1. In WordPress, open **Tools → Export**, choose **All content** and press **Download Export File** (an `.xml` file).
2. In Cleitext, open **Import from WordPress**, choose the file, the language of the content (on multilingual sites), the role of the imported authors and which comments to bring, and press **Import**.
3. The import runs in the background: you can leave the page. While it runs the page shows the step it is on (reading the file, media, posts and pages, menus) and how many media files and posts are done out of how many; **Cancel** stops it after the current item, keeping what was imported. At the end a report shows what was created, what was already there, and what was adapted or left out. The report is in the language of the admin (since 1.32.1: English or Italian), also the lines written by the core and by other plugins.

Large exports (since 1.27.0, core 0.82): the upload is answered at once and the file is read and imported as a background job, so a large file on a slow connection no longer fails with a server error, and the hooks of other plugins it calls (Fresco, Prisma, Campus…) have the longer time limit of background jobs (`PLUGIN_JOB_TIMEOUT_MS`, 5 minutes) instead of the 5 seconds of a page. Only one import runs on a site at a time: starting another says which file is being imported and by whom. If the server restarts during an import, after two minutes the page says so instead of showing it as running forever: import the same file again, and only what is missing is added.

Scripts can do the same with the API (`/api/v1/ext/wordpress-importer`): `POST /imports` (multipart `file`, options in the query) answers `202` with `{ job }`, whose `id` is also at `GET /imports/<id>`; `GET /status` gives the job of the site (`status`: `running`, `done`, `failed` or `cancelled`; `counts`: `phase`, `parsed`, `media` and `entries` as `{ done, total }`; `report` at the end); `POST /cancel` cancels it and `DELETE /status` clears a finished one.

## What it imports

Posts, pages, images and other media, categories, tags, menus and authors, with dates, drafts, parent pages and featured images. Block content stays made of blocks; classic content (and plain HTML or text widgets) is split into paragraphs and headings like WordPress does, keeping bold, italics, underlines and colors written as inline styles, and the size of a paragraph written all in one size. The old addresses (`/2024/05/my-post/`, `/?p=12`) redirect to the new pages.

- Block settings of recent WordPress versions stay: text alignment, preset text sizes, rows and grids of groups (flex and grid layouts), columns aligned vertically or kept side by side on phones.
- **Covers** become a group with the picture followed by the content; with **Prisma** active they become a container with the picture as background, the overlay, the height and the position of the content, as in WordPress.
- Pages built with **Elementor** become **Fresco** pages: sections, columns and containers, the widgets (free, Pro and atomic) with their texts, links, images, global colors and fonts, typography, spacing, per-device values, hover states, motion effects and dynamic tags, and the page settings. Links of the widgets to the old site become addresses of this site, as in menus; full-height sections and containers keep their height. Widgets with no Fresco counterpart keep their text and are listed in the report. Activate Fresco to show and edit them.
- Pages built with **Divi** (since 1.23.0) become **Fresco** pages too, laid out as Divi lays them out: sections (also specialty and fullwidth ones), rows and columns with Divi's widths, gutters, equal heights and spacing, their margins and paddings per device (negative ones too), backgrounds (colors, images, parallax, gradients, the gradient over an image), borders, box shadows (Divi's presets and their changes), section dividers, and the modules: text (fonts, colors and sizes of the text, its headings, links and lists), heading, image, button, blurb, divider and spacer, gallery (grid or slider), icon, code, post title, fullwidth header and menu. Divi's icons become the closest Font Awesome icons, global colors and module presets are kept, dynamic links to pages become their addresses, and a page's CSS that set the columns of its galleries sets them in Fresco. Modules with no counterpart keep their text and are listed in the report. Pages show the whole width, without the theme's title and spacing, as in Divi.
- With a Divi site, also upload Divi's **theme options** (`et_divi.json`: the `et_divi` option, or Divi → Theme Options → export) in the optional field next to the export: they bring the global colors, the fonts, the module presets, the content width and the buttons' style, which go into Fresco's site settings (with Divi's breakpoints). Without them the report says so and Divi's own defaults are used.
- Divi's **Theme Builder** (header, body and footer layouts of the active templates, with their conditions) and **Library** (saved sections, rows and modules, global ones too) become **Fresco Pro** templates. Without Fresco Pro, Fresco shows the imported header and footer in the theme's place, on the pages the templates chose (Fresco → Tools lists and removes them); the menu of the header's menu module gets the location `primary`.
- **WooCommerce** products become **Cleishop** products (activate Cleishop before importing): title, description, short description, picture and gallery, categories and brand, price and sale with its dates, SKU and GTIN, stock, sizes and weight, tax status, featured and catalog visibility, related products, attributes with their values and variations with their own prices, codes, stock and pictures. Orders and coupons are counted and not imported.
- Elementor's **kit** goes to Fresco's site settings, and its **Theme Builder templates** and popups, custom code and custom fonts to **Fresco Pro** (activate it before importing to keep the templates).
- **Colors and fonts of Elementor widgets** (since 1.26.0, Fresco 2.11.4): Elementor's **Disable Default Colors** and **Disable Default Fonts** (Elementor → Settings) are WordPress options, not in the export. Choose **From the theme** if they were on: widgets without their own colors and fonts take the theme's (Fresco's **Theme's colors by default** and **Theme's fonts by default** are turned on), while the kit's global colors and fonts are still imported for the widgets that chose them. **Elementor's kit** applies the kit's as defaults. **Automatic** (the default; `?elementorDefaults=auto|kit|theme` on the upload) takes the theme's when the kit still has Elementor's own system colors (a kit never set up) and the theme is not Hello, and says so in the report.
- Sites translated with **WPML** or **Polylang** (since 1.28.0): on a site with several languages (Multilingual plugin), the site's languages are used by default, also by scripts that do not send `?languages=`. The language of each page and post comes from the export when it says it: WPML's data (`_wpml_import_language_code` and `_wpml_import_translation_group` of WPML's Export and Import, or `wpml_language`, `language_code`, `trid`, `_icl_lang_duplicate_of`, a map of translations in `_icl_translations`), Polylang's `language` and `post_translations` terms, or addresses starting with the language (`/en/rooms/`, `?lang=en`, whose translation is the page at the same address without it). Pages that translate each other are imported in their language and linked as translations, also when they have the same slug (`/camere/` and `/en/camere/`). Exports of WP-CLI, which say nothing about languages, still work as before: translations share the main page's address and their language is guessed from their text. Pages in a language the site does not have (or that was unchecked) are left out and counted in the report: add the language and import the file again. The upload page reads the file before sending it and shows the languages found (and how many pages each), with the site's languages to import, checked for those found; the content language is set to the export's main one.
- **Menus of other languages** (since 1.28.0): WP-CLI's export of a WPML site has only the main language's menus as terms, but every menu item names its menu: menus are rebuilt from their items too, each in the language of most of its pages, and an item linking a page of another language links its translation. A language with translated pages and no menu gets a copy of the main language's menus, each page replaced by its translation (with the translation's title); items without a translation are left out, links and labels of other items stay as they were. The report says which menus were rebuilt.
- **Language switchers** (since 1.29.0): Polylang's language switcher in a menu (a custom link to `#pll_switcher`) becomes Multilingual's switcher link (`#language-switcher`), with the same choices: dropdown or one item per language, codes instead of names, the page's language left out, languages without a translation hidden. With **Multilingual** 1.6.0 active during the import, WPML's and Polylang's switcher shortcodes (`[wpml_language_selector_widget]`, `[wpml_language_selector_footer]`, `[wpml_language_switcher]`, `[polylang_langswitcher]`), their blocks and their legacy widgets become its **Language switcher** block. WPML's own placements (in a menu, in the footer) are settings that WordPress exports do not carry: choose them in Languages → Language switcher.
- **Embeds** (since 1.32.0): WordPress's Embed blocks (also the older `core-embed/…` blocks, their saved `<figure class="wp-block-embed">`, `[embed]…[/embed]` and addresses alone on their line that WordPress embeds by itself) become Cleitext's Embed block: YouTube, Vimeo, Dailymotion, Spotify, TikTok (videos and profiles), Instagram (posts and reels), Facebook (posts, videos, pages) and X (posts) show their player, held by the Privacy plugin until consent; Instagram and X profiles and other sites become a link card. For other providers WordPress embeds (SoundCloud, Reddit, Flickr…) the HTML WordPress cached for them (`_oembed_…`) is kept in an HTML block, its scripts held until consent with the Privacy plugin. Social posts pasted as their provider's code (TikTok's, Instagram's or X's blockquote with its script, Facebook's `fb-post`, a known player's iframe) become the Embed block too. Each embed is listed in the report.
- **Shortcodes** standing alone (on their own line or paragraph, in a Shortcode block, in an Elementor shortcode, text or HTML widget) become the blocks of an active plugin that knows them (plugin API 1.36, `import.shortcode`), e.g. `[newsletter_form]` → NewLetter's form, `[ssa_booking]` → Agenda's booking; the others stay as text and are counted in the report. Since 1.32.1 an enclosing shortcode may span several lines, blank ones included, and hold other shortcodes: The Newsletter Plugin's `[newsletter_form]…[newsletter_field …]…[/newsletter_form]` is read whole, in a Shortcode block, in a paragraph and in classic content, and so is `[embed]` with its address on a line of its own.
- **Newsletter forms** (since 1.30.0): forms of email services pasted in HTML blocks or in the classic editor (Brevo's `sibforms.com` forms and iframes, Mailchimp's `list-manage.com` embedded forms, MailerLite, Kit/ConvertKit and ActiveCampaign) are offered to the active plugins: with the **NewLetter** plugin active they become its signup form, with the Mailchimp list when the form says it; so do the shortcodes and blocks of Mailchimp for WP, The Newsletter Plugin, MailPoet and Brevo's plugin. Without it, the HTML is kept as before (in the classic editor it is kept as an HTML block instead of being lost). The report counts them by service.
- **The addresses of WordPress** (since 1.31.0): WordPress exports do not carry the permalink settings, but every post's link is its real address, so the structure of the posts is read from them (`/%postname%/` → `/{slug}`, `/%year%/%monthnum%/%postname%/` → `/{year}/{month}/{slug}`), and the first part of category and tag archives from the links to them in the content and in menus (`/topics/news/`, or `/news/` with the base removed). On a site without posts, categories or tags yet they are used (cores with plugin API 1.55), so the posts keep their addresses and need no redirect. A site that already has some keeps its own and the report says what differs, unless “Use the addresses of WordPress also if this site already has posts” is checked on the upload page (`?permalinks=replace`): then its current addresses redirect. The upload page shows the posts' structure found in the file. Structures Cleitext has no equivalent for (`%day%`, `%post_id%`, `%category%`) are reported, and their old addresses redirect.
- **Contact Form 7** (since 1.28.0): every form becomes a **Forms** block where its shortcode was (`[contact-form-7 id="…"]`, by the post's id, by CF7 5.8's hash id or by `title`), also inside Elementor pages: text, email, phone, web address, number (and range), date, long text, menus (several choices become checkboxes), checkboxes, radio buttons and consents, required ones, placeholders, default values (`default:2` too), minimum and maximum, extra classes, labels taken from the markup around each field (`<label>`, the paragraph or line before it), the button's text, the email subject (fields as `{name}`, `[_site_title]` as the site's title), Reply-To from the additional headers and the thank-you message. On a multilingual site each page shows the form of its language (the WPML translation of the form). What Forms does not do is reported per form: quizzes (the Captcha plugin protects forms instead), file uploads, hidden and other plugins' fields, a recipient other than the site's (Forms sends to the address of Settings → Forms) and the autoresponder (Mail (2)); the mail's body is replaced by Forms' list of every field. Activate Forms to show them; a shortcode whose form is not in the export stays as text.
- **Cookie banners' leftovers** (since 1.28.0): CookieYes, Complianz and Cookiebot leave CSS rules (`a.cky-banner-element…`), `<style>` blocks, scripts (Cookiebot's declaration) and "Cookie settings" links in pages, HTML blocks and the Additional CSS. The Privacy plugin replaces those banners: their rules, style blocks and scripts are removed, their settings links become links to `#cookie-preferences`, which open the Privacy plugin's preferences (or those of the consent manager chosen there), and HTML blocks left empty go. Only these managers' own class names, ids and scripts are touched; the report says what was removed.
- Blocks of other plugins that show posts filtered by category or tag keep their filter: the WordPress term ids become the imported terms.
- The Customizer's **Additional CSS** (since 1.24.0) goes to **Appearance → Customize → Additional CSS**, for every theme: the CSS of the export's theme, or the most recently changed when the export has several (the others are named in the report). It is added after the CSS the site already has, under a comment, and not added again when the same export is imported twice. Since 1.26.0 its braces are matched first (WordPress printed it alone, where the browser closes a block left open; here CSS comes after it): an empty block left open at the end, such as `@media (max-width: 580px){`, is removed, one with rules is closed, a `}` with no `{` is removed, and the report says so.
- Since core 0.83 (plugin API 1.53) the Additional CSS also follows the class names of active plugins that render other plugins' blocks under their own (**Prisma** for Spectra: `.uagb-ifb-separator` becomes `.pr-info-box__separator`), in selectors only, and the report lists each rewrite.
- The imported site stays **light** (since 1.25.0, core 0.81): WordPress themes rarely have a dark mode, so when the active theme offers a colour scheme (Aurora's **Colour scheme**) and the site has not chosen one, it is set to light. It can be changed in Customize.
- Nothing already on the site is overwritten: importing the same file twice adds nothing new. Posts, pages and media imported before are recognised by their WordPress id (since 1.27.0, core 0.82; by type, language, address and title for those imported by older versions): they are kept as they are, media are not downloaded again, and posts get only the comments they lack; menus with the same name are not added again. Two posts with the same title are never taken for one.
- Long addresses stay as they were (since 1.27.0, core 0.82): slugs keep up to 200 characters, as in WordPress, instead of being cut at 80 (two posts whose addresses differed only after the 80th character became one). A slug cut at the limit that is already taken gets a suffix within it (`…-2`), and its old address redirects to the new one.
- Media files are downloaded from the WordPress site, only from public addresses: keep the old site online until the import ends.
- Authors get an account without a password, as members of the site with the chosen role; they set a password with **Forgot password**.
- Comments come along with their threads: the approved ones by default, or all of them (also those waiting for approval and spam), or none, for sites whose comments were closed and only old spam is left (the **Comments** choice; `?comments=approved|all|skip` on the upload). Pingbacks, trackbacks and trashed comments are never imported; what was left out is counted in the report.
- WordPress addresses end with a slash (`/my-page/`): when the export's do, the report suggests turning on **End addresses with a slash** in **Settings → Content structure**, so the addresses stay exactly the same (nothing changes by itself). Custom post types of other plugins are counted and not imported; shortcodes stay as text.

- Custom fields (for example **ACF**'s): the meta of every post, page and term, and ACF's own field groups and fields, go to the active plugins that read them (plugin API 1.48, `import.wxrMeta`), which turn them into field groups and values; without such a plugin they are left out, as before. Since 1.22.0 (plugin API 1.49) they also get the export's authors, for fields that name people, and the slugs given to custom post types and taxonomies.

Only administrators can use it (capability `import_wordpress`).

Plugin API 1.8: it reads the WordPress export itself and imports the result with `ctx.transfer.importDocument` (permission `import`); the upload comes through `request.file()`.
